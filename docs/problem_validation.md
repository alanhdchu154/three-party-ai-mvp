# Problem Validation

**Last updated**: 2026-05-21

This product is not just a chatbot, wellness dashboard, or tutoring funnel. It is a privacy-preserving truth capture and support coordination layer.

## Core Problem

Most people do not reveal sensitive truths to other people early enough.

By the time a student can tell a parent, teacher, counselor, or tutor directly what is really happening, the hardest internal threshold may already have been crossed. The earlier problem is not always lack of support. It is lack of a safe first listener.

For online high school students, this is sharper:

- The school sees grades, assignments, attendance, and parent complaints.
- Teachers see class participation and work quality.
- Parents see mood, sleep, conflict, and anxiety at home.
- Students know the private reason something feels impossible, but may not say it directly.

The support system receives fragments, late signals, and distorted interpretations.

## Product Hypothesis

If students, parents, and teachers each have a private AI space, they may disclose more honest context earlier.

If a coordinator can translate those private signals into privacy-safe patterns, adults can act earlier without exposing raw secrets or breaking trust.

## What We Are Solving

### 1. Truth Capture

Can AI become a lower-friction first listener for difficult truths?

Evidence to seek:

- Student says something to AI that they have not said directly to adults.
- Parent admits fear, confusion, guilt, or pressure they would not say in a school meeting.
- Teacher records a soft observation earlier than they would escalate formally.

### 2. Privacy-Preserving Translation

Can the system convert private disclosures into safe, useful guidance?

Evidence to seek:

- Parent/teacher reports contain no raw quote, secret, or reconstructable event.
- Student feels the system did not betray them.
- Adult recipients understand what to do without demanding the hidden detail.

### 3. Support Routing

Can the system identify what kind of support is appropriate next?

Possible routes:

- Student self-reflection and check-in
- Teacher adjustment
- Parent communication coaching
- School human review
- External counseling or crisis handoff
- Academic tutoring or 1-on-1 support through 杰尼

杰尼 is one possible route, not the default destination.

## Customer Hypotheses

### GIIS / Online High School

Buyer: school owner, school leader, student success lead.

Why buy:

- Online schools lack informal visibility.
- Parent trust and retention depend on whether families feel the school sees the student.
- Early support can prevent avoidable withdrawal, conflict, or academic collapse.

Success metric:

- Earlier detection of support needs.
- Better parent/teacher/student coordination.
- Improved retention and parent trust.

### Families / B2C

Buyer: parent, student, or individual user.

Why buy:

- People want a safe place to say what they cannot yet say to another person.
- Families need help translating tension into a calmer conversation.

Risk:

- Parents may think they are buying a tool to discover the child's secrets.
- This must be rejected clearly.

Safe framing:

- The product does not reveal secrets.
- It helps the person who disclosed decide what they may want to share.
- It can generate a sanitized summary only with explicit consent.

### Tutoring / 1-on-1 Organizations

Buyer: tutoring operator, academic support lead.

Why buy:

- Tutors often enter a case with too little context.
- Academic issues are often mixed with motivation, family pressure, identity, confidence, or planning problems.

Success metric:

- Better intake briefings.
- Faster fit between student need and tutor support.
- Higher conversion only when the case is actually appropriate for 1-on-1.

## Validation Metrics

### Must Prove Before v1.0

1. Truth capture:
   - At least 2 cases where the student discloses meaningful support context not visible in ordinary school data.

2. Privacy preservation:
   - 0 raw quote / raw secret / reconstructable private event leaks in parent-safe and teacher-safe outputs.

3. Adult actionability:
   - Parent/teacher/reviewer can name the next action after reading the summary.

4. Reviewer agreement:
   - Human reviewer agrees with support route in most high-concern cases.

5. Trust not harmed:
   - Student does not feel punished, exposed, or tricked after AI-mediated support.

6. Routing quality:
   - At least one case is correctly routed to non-tutoring support.
   - At least one case is evaluated for whether academic 1-on-1 is or is not justified.

## What Not To Claim Yet

- Do not claim clinical improvement.
- Do not claim mental health diagnosis.
- Do not claim the AI detects all risk.
- Do not claim synthetic Saga A validates real-world behavior.
- Do not claim every support need should route to 杰尼.

## Next Validation Step

Run a 1-2 family GIIS micro-pilot with explicit consent, human review, and local/private provider boundaries.

The pilot should test the trust loop:

private disclosure -> privacy-safe translation -> adult action -> student still feels safe.
