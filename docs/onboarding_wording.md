# Pilot Onboarding Wording

**Status**: v0.9 internal draft  
**Use**: Alan can adapt this for the first GIIS family conversation.

## One-Minute Parent Explanation

We are testing a small GIIS support tool that helps students, parents, and teachers each share what they are seeing in a lower-pressure way.

Your child can talk to a student AI privately. You can also talk to a parent AI about your concerns. A coordinator then looks for support patterns, but it does not show your child's raw words to you, teachers, or tutors.

The goal is not to hide things from parents. The goal is to make sure students can be honest early, without feeling that every word will be repeated back to adults. If there is a serious safety concern, a trusted human reviewer must step in.

This is not counseling, diagnosis, or emergency care. It is a small support pilot to help GIIS understand what kind of help a student may need next.

## Parent Expectations

Parents should expect:

- broad support themes;
- suggested ways to respond;
- what to avoid saying or doing;
- whether school review or other support is recommended.

Parents should not expect:

- raw student transcripts;
- secret-by-secret summaries;
- proof that the student said a specific thing;
- AI diagnosis;
- AI replacement for urgent human help.

## Student Explanation

This AI is a place where you can talk more honestly than you might in a parent meeting or class conversation.

Your raw words are not shown to your parents or teachers. The system may summarize general themes, like "the student feels overwhelmed by pressure" or "the student may need more control over planning," but it should not reveal your exact words or private details.

If there is a serious safety concern, a trusted adult at GIIS may need to review the situation. The AI should not promise unlimited secrecy in those cases.

You can stop participating.

## Teacher / GIIS Adult Explanation

This pilot helps GIIS combine three perspectives:

- what the student is willing to tell AI;
- what the parent is worried about;
- what the teacher or GIIS adult observes.

Teacher-safe outputs should focus on educational support:

- engagement;
- workload;
- planning;
- participation;
- communication style;
- when to escalate for human review.

Teacher outputs should not contain private student or family details unless they are necessary and authorized.

## What To Say If Parent Asks For Raw Student Conversation

We cannot provide that.

The whole reason this tool can work is that the student has a protected space. If we turn it into a transcript-sharing tool, students will stop telling the truth, and the support value collapses.

What we can provide is a privacy-safe summary of support patterns and suggested next steps.

## What To Say If Parent Worries AI Is Hiding Things

The AI is not replacing parent authority or school responsibility.

It is creating a safer first step so students can share difficult things earlier. Serious safety concerns are not ignored. They are routed to human review.

The boundary is: parents get support guidance, not raw secrets.

## What To Say If Student Worries About Being Betrayed

You are right to ask.

The system is designed so your exact words are not repeated to parents or teachers. It summarizes themes only. If something suggests immediate safety risk, an adult may need to step in, but even then the goal is to use the minimum necessary information.

You can ask what would be shared before anything is sent.

## Short Public Positioning

GIIS is testing an AI Student Success & Support Layer.

It helps students, families, and teachers surface support needs earlier, while protecting private disclosures and keeping human adults responsible for serious decisions.

## Phrases To Avoid

- "We can see what your child is really thinking."
- "The AI will tell parents what students hide."
- "This detects mental health problems."
- "This replaces counseling."
- "This automatically routes students to tutoring."
- "Everything is confidential no matter what."

## Better Phrases

- "Private by default, shareable only by consent or safety necessity."
- "Support, not surveillance."
- "We summarize patterns, not secrets."
- "The goal is earlier help without breaking trust."
- "The right support, from the right person, at the right time."
